# CRYPTO 2026

This file records the latest published CRYPTO main-conference rules verified on the date in the frontmatter. CRYPTO 2026 submission is closed; do not assume its special conflict-of-interest or presentation policies apply to CRYPTO 2027 without real-time verification.

## Hard constraints

- Submit an anonymous PDF in LaTeX using Springer's LNCS format. Do not alter LNCS default fonts, sizes, or margins, and display page numbers (for example, `\pagestyle{plain}`).
- Keep the main body at **30 pages or fewer, excluding bibliography**. Clearly marked supplementary material may be an appendix or separate files, but reviewers need not read it and the submission must be intelligible without it.
- Begin with a title, short abstract, introduction, and technical overview that contains the main ideas of the paper.
- Do not put author names, affiliations, or obvious identifying references in the PDF. Enter authors separately; they are visible to the program co-chairs but not the rest of the PC until acceptance.
- Do not substantially duplicate published work or work submitted in parallel to another journal, conference, or workshop with proceedings.
- Follow IACR COI requirements in HotCRP. CRYPTO 2026 used a stronger-than-standard policy: recent joint publications/collaborations within two years and close personal relationships were automatic conflicts. Treat this as **2026-specific** and recheck later editions.
- Before submission, real-time verify the active edition's exact LNCS version, page limit, anonymity, AI-use, COI, and presentation rules.

## Writing and review priorities

- State the cryptographic problem, adversarial model, assumptions, and exact theorem/security property before detailed construction or proof.
- Use the technical overview to make the central idea intelligible before the formal development; keep all material needed to evaluate correctness in the main paper.
- Make reductions, parameter choices, asymptotic/ concrete bounds, computational assumptions, and limitations explicit.
- Separate proof-backed guarantees from implementation, benchmarking, or heuristic observations. Do not overstate a construction's applicability beyond its model.
- Use supplementary material for secondary details or prior-review responses, never as the only support for the central claim.
