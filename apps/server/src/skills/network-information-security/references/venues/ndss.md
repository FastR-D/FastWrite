# NDSS 2026

This file records the latest verified NDSS main-conference rules on the date in the frontmatter. The 2026 call is closed; real-time verify the next edition's format, cycle, authorship, and revision rules before treating any item below as current.

## Hard constraints

- Submit an English PDF using the NDSS template on US letter (not A4) paper in two columns. Use Times at 10 point or larger, 11-point or larger leading, and columns no larger than 9.25 by 3.5 inches.
- Keep technical content to **13 pages or fewer**, excluding `Ethics Considerations`, references, and appendices. Place any optional `Ethics Considerations` section immediately before references. Reviewers are not required to read appendices.
- Ensure the PDF and figures render correctly in Adobe Reader and remain legible in black and white.
- Preserve anonymity and follow the submission system's authorship and conflict rules. For 2026, no author could be added after the deadline, and each author could appear on at most six submissions per cycle (12 total); these limits require real-time verification for a later edition.
- Do not widely advertise the paper during review. The 2026 call also prohibited major overlap from a summer-cycle rejection into its fall cycle.
- Before submission, real-time verify the active review cycle, mandatory registration fields, current template, author cap, revision/resubmission policy, and ethics requirements.

## Writing and review priorities

- Anchor the paper in network or systems security with a clear relation to real systems, networks, or applications. The 2026 call flags purely theoretical cryptography, mathematical proofs without implementation/evaluation, primarily AI/ML work, and non-security performance work as poor fit.
- Write the abstract and introduction for a general security researcher: make the practical problem, system context, threat model, contribution, and evidence compelling and accessible.
- Prioritize deployable system design, implementation detail, empirical evaluation, and the security/efficiency/usability/ethics tradeoffs relevant to the claimed contribution.
- An NDSS knowledge-systematization paper must go beyond a survey by examining existing approaches, identifying gaps/limitations, and supplying insights or evidence; `SoK:` is optional, not required.
- Keep the main body sufficient for review; do not place essential validation, assumptions, or evidence only in appendices.
