# Bioinformatics

## Hard constraints

- Select the manuscript type before budgeting: Original Papers are up to 7 template pages, while Application Notes are up to 4; other types have separate rules. Format-free initial submission is allowed, but excessive length can be returned without review.
- Peer review is single-anonymized, so author identities remain in the manuscript. A cover letter is required and must explain suitability and any deviation from the instructions.
- For software or novel algorithms, provide a usable implementation, tests, documentation, stable non-commercial access, and appropriate real biological data. Machine-learning studies require an independent test set and must address leakage or homology where relevant.
- Make supporting data public in stable repositories subject to ethical and legal constraints. Disclose AI use, funding, conflicts, related work, preprints, and reused-material permissions under current OUP policy.

## Writing and review priorities

- Use the manuscript-type section structure. For Original Papers, plan the title page, structured abstract, introduction, system and methods, algorithm, implementation, discussion, and references.
- State biological relevance, data provenance, preprocessing, independent validation, statistical uncertainty, availability, limitations, and reproducibility without overstating generality.
- Verify bibliographic records and confirm that biomedical and computational claims are supported by the cited primary evidence; check known corrections or retractions.

## Planning checklist

- Choose Original Paper, Application Note, or another type before enforcing its page/word/figure budget; do not apply one global numeric limit.
- Check the cover letter, structured abstract fields, availability statement, public data/code, independent test set, ethics, funding, conflicts, permissions, and AI disclosure.
- Compile LaTeX locally before upload and recheck the live OUP guide at submission and revision stages.
