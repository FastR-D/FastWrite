# Journal of Machine Learning Research (JMLR)

## Hard constraints

- Submit only original work that is not simultaneously under review at a conference or another journal. Preprints are permitted under JMLR's policy.
- Typeset the submission in LaTeX using the official JMLR style file; submissions outside the JMLR style can be rejected without review. Submit PDF as required by the journal.
- Include a cover letter that discloses materially overlapping prior work, confirms all authors' consent, declares conflicts, suggests 3–5 suitable action editors and 3–5 reviewers without conflicts, and lists keywords.
- The title page must include the corresponding author's complete contact information, a running title of at most 50 characters, five keywords, and an abstract of at most 200 words.
- Papers over 35 pages, including appendices, may take longer to review or be rejected if reviewers cannot be found; papers over 50 pages require a cover-letter justification and may be desk rejected.

## Writing and review priorities

- Present a complete, principled machine-learning contribution with sound empirical validation and/or theoretical justification, not an application-only report.
- State claims clearly and support them with analysis or reproducible experiments; report what was learned, not merely what was built.
- Situate the work in the broad ML literature and explain why new terminology, tasks, or techniques are needed.
- For systems and applications, expose the general principle or insight; for theory, discuss practical utility where appropriate.
- Prefer a self-contained archival treatment, moving only genuinely auxiliary material to online appendices.

## Planning checklist

- [ ] Budget pages including appendices; if exceeding 35 or 50 pages, reduce material or prepare the required cover-letter explanation.
- [ ] Use the unmodified official JMLR style and validate that the PDF renders correctly.
- [ ] Keep the abstract at or below 200 words; provide five keywords and a running title no longer than 50 characters.
- [ ] Prepare the required cover letter, including overlap, consent, conflicts, editor/reviewer suggestions, and keywords.
- [ ] Verify all central claims have empirical or theoretical support and that experiments can be reproduced.
