# Target conference or journal specialization

The selected venue file is the target-specific authority bundled with FastWrite. Its edition, track, and manuscript stage control which rules apply. Official author instructions and the downloaded official template outrank community advice, older templates, and generic publisher defaults.

## SHOULD DO

- Confirm the venue edition, paper track, and stage before writing; translate each applicable hard rule into a checkable task.
- Start from the selected template when one is available. Preserve its class/style files, document options, margins, fonts, line numbering, bibliography style, and required metadata.
- Use the venue's expected argument and evidence conventions only when they improve the actual paper: for example, proofs and explicit assumptions in theory, threat models and responsible disclosure in security, artifact and systems methodology in systems, or participant protocol and positionality where relevant in HCI.
- Keep material required for review in the main paper when the venue says reviewers need not inspect appendices or supplements.
- Re-run format, page, anonymity, comment, cross-reference, and citation-integrity checks after any change that can affect layout or evidence.
- Treat a publisher-family template as a starting point only. Confirm the exact journal or event options before submission.

## SHOULDN'T DO

- Do not combine page limits, anonymity rules, tracks, or style files from different years, venues, or manuscript stages.
- Do not edit `.cls`, `.sty`, margins, font sizes, spacing, caption layout, bibliography compression, or PDF geometry to gain space unless the current official instructions explicitly permit it.
- Do not treat a community mirror as proof that a template is current or official. Follow its provenance link and compare against the current author guide.
- Do not copy wording, claims, experiments, or citations from example papers or template instruction text. Examples teach structure and required fields, not manuscript content.
- Do not move central evidence to supplementary material merely to meet the page limit, and do not rely on reviewers reading optional appendices.
- Do not mark a venue rule or citation as verified when the check only found a plausible match or an inaccessible page.

## Language adaptation

Adopt the target field's established terminology and section roles, but keep the prose direct. Venue specialization must not become imitation of stock phrases, famous papers, reviewer jargon, or an invented “conference voice.” Exact technical meaning, evidence, and readable sentences take precedence over stylistic mimicry.
